Philipp Rieber

A technical leader and hands-on software architect. Twenty years of software development, now amplified by AI agents.

Staff Software Engineer at ottonova · Munich, Germany

Background

Online since the mid-nineties, I put my first website up in 2000 and taught myself PHP while studying physics in Berlin and Paris. I built sites for small businesses, started writing about it, and today build enterprise-grade software and enjoy teaching others how to do it.

Full stack

Until 2014 · Onemedia, web agency

Seven years at a Munich web agency, from web developer to team lead and deputy managing director, building web applications for the automotive industry. I did a bit of everything, from servers and databases to the conversations with customers and stakeholders that show what is worth building. I kept writing books and articles and started speaking at PHP and Symfony meetups. I also contributed to the documentation of Symfony, still my favorite framework today.

Backend & architecture

2014 – 2025 · ottonova, health insurance

Around 2014 I got into domain-driven design, hexagonal architecture and microservices, which changed how I think about software. After a short detour through my own insurance idea, the agency Inviqa and the payments company Paymill, I joined ottonova in 2016 as one of a handful of engineers, starting with an empty repository. We built a private health insurer from scratch, today a mid-size company with over 200 employees and tens of thousands of customers.

I spent about two and a half years each as senior backend engineer and team lead, and the cross-functional teams we set up along Team Topologies are still a cornerstone of how we work. Then I returned to the technology as the company’s first Staff Engineer, an enabling role across the whole stack and the whole company: I spot what slows people down and improve developer experience and productivity.

Agentic coding

2025 – today

I started early with AI as a helper in chats and IDE integrations, then went deep on Claude Code and agentic coding. Since the beginning of 2026 I hardly write code by hand. Claude Code and its ecosystem are my everyday tools across the whole stack: from frontend to infrastructure, planning and research, in PHP, JavaScript, Go, Terraform and more.

So I feel like a full stack engineer again, only differently. The craft has moved towards context engineering: what the agent knows, what it may do, and how I check its work. It is the biggest productivity boost of my career, and I keep refining how I work with it. Software engineering is being reinvented, and I’m enthusiastic and intrigued to be part of it.
