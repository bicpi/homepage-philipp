Philipp Rieber

A technical leader and hands-on software architect. Twenty years in web development, now embracing agentic coding.

Staff Software Engineer at ottonova · Munich, Germany

Background

I grew up in Munich and have been online since the mid-nineties. My first website followed in 2000. I taught myself to program with PHP while studying physics in Berlin and Paris, built sites for small businesses on the side, and kept going: today I build enterprise-grade software and enjoy teaching others how to do it.

Full stack

Until 2014 · Onemedia, web agency

My first job, which I held for seven years, was at a Munich web agency building web applications for the automotive industry. I started as a web developer and grew into team lead and deputy managing director. I did a bit of everything: servers, code, design, databases, deployments, and the conversations with customers and stakeholders that show what is actually worth building. I also began writing books and articles and speaking at PHP and Symfony meetups.

Backend & architecture

2014 – 2025 · ottonova, health insurance

Around 2014 I got into domain-driven design, hexagonal architecture and microservices, which changed how I think about software. After a short tour through an insurance start-up idea, the agency Inviqa and the payments company Paymill, I joined ottonova in 2016 as one of a handful of engineers starting with an empty repository. Together with many colleagues from engineering and the insurance business we built a private health insurer from scratch, including a flexible document printing system that produces millions of customer documents and our messaging system. As the tooling landscape exploded, I focused on backends, databases and integrating partners.

I spent about two and a half years as a senior backend engineer and as many as team lead, which I enjoyed more than expected; the cross-functional teams we set up along Team Topologies still work well. Then I missed the technology and became the company’s first Staff Engineer, an enabling role: I listen to what people say and write, look for what slows them down, and improve developer experience and productivity wherever I find room for it.

Agentic coding

2025 – today

I started early with AI as a helper in chats and IDE integrations, then went deep on Claude Code, agentic coding and agent harnesses. Since the beginning of 2026 I hardly write code by hand. Claude Code, with MCP servers and custom skills around it, is my everyday tool across the whole stack: frontend, backend, infrastructure, planning and research, in PHP, JavaScript, Go, Terraform and whatever else the job needs.

So I feel like a full stack engineer again, only differently. The craft has moved towards context engineering: what the agent knows, what it may do, and how I check its work. It is the biggest productivity boost of my career, and I keep refining how I work with it. Software engineering is being reinvented, and I’m enthusiastic and intrigued to be part of it.

Approach

  • Product first. I care about the business behind the software; talking to the people who use it teaches me more than the specification.
  • Why before how. I question requirements and dig until I understand the real need.
  • Writing and teaching. Documenting, mentoring and sponsoring have been part of every job.
  • Always learning. Agile, DevOps, domain-driven design and now agents.
